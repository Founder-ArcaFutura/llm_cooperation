COOPERATION BEFORE ALIGNMENT?
An exploratory multiple-choice strategy-selection screen, followed by conditional mechanistic tests
Experimental specification v0.2  |  3 November 2026  |  Adrian Hau / Arca Futura LLC
Status: exploratory protocol proposed; no data collected or preregistration completed. Phase 0 is a deliberately small behavioural screen. A positive result licenses better-controlled follow-up, not a mechanistic claim. Later phases remain conditional and must be frozen before confirmatory held-out evaluation.
Executive summary
Central question: before supervised fine-tuning or preference optimization, do base language models preferentially select a cooperative strategy when it is one of several plausible actions? First run a small, balanced multiple-choice behavioural screen across three or four pretrained models. Only if it yields an interpretable pattern should we undertake the larger programme: matched base/instruct comparison, social-game controls, residual-stream probes, and causal interventions.
Core distinction: a base model can encode descriptions of cooperation without preferring it, and can prefer agreeable-sounding wording without selecting cooperative actions. Phase 0 asks only about strategy-selection behaviour; it does not demonstrate innate agency, motives, altruism, or a weight-level mechanism. Later phases test whether any observed preference is causally supported by identifiable representations.
1. Motivation, related work, and prospective contribution
Prior work has identified SAE features associated with altruistic allocations and used causal steering in an instruction-tuned model; other studies trace moral concepts and sycophancy-related representations. Studies of repeated prisoner’s dilemmas show cooperation can depend strongly on framing, payoff structure, and opponent context. These results make the question tractable but do not establish a pretrained, cross-game cooperation mechanism. See the source register in §12.
The motivating observation from Adrian’s earlier cooperation/StratEval probes is that some models repeatedly accepted cooperative offers even when successful reciprocal performance appeared infeasible. That is a hypothesis generator, not an independently established finding for this protocol. Its strongest competing explanation is acquiescence or sycophancy. We will not use those historical prompts in the confirmatory corpus.
Prospective contribution: first establish—or fail to establish—a position-robust cooperative strategy-selection bias in multiple pretrained families. If warranted, investigate whether such behaviour reflects a transferable pretrained representation distinct from generic agreement, altruism, fairness, and instruction compliance.
2. Research questions and staged hypotheses
H1 — Representational availability
At one or more pre-choice residual-stream positions, held-out cooperative vs noncooperative choices will be decodable above a label-permutation baseline in a pretrained base checkpoint, after controlling for game type, payoff values, response labels, and lexical framing. This demonstrates predictive information, not yet a cooperative disposition.
H2 — Causal relevance (primary mechanistic test)
Intervening on a representation identified using discovery data will cause a directionally consistent change in held-out cooperative-choice probability in the base model, beyond matched random-vector and task-competence controls. Both positive and negative interventions are attempted.
H3 — Training-stage comparison
A matched instruct checkpoint will retain, attenuate, amplify, or reconfigure the identified cooperative signals. We estimate the change rather than predetermining its sign. A base-versus-instruct difference does not by itself identify whether supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, data mixture, or other post-training stages caused it.
H0 — Cooperative strategy-selection default (primary Phase 0, exploratory)
Under minimally moralized, balanced one-shot scenarios in which cooperate, act independently, compete/exploit, and disengage are all feasible options, base checkpoints may select cooperation more often than the alternatives. Estimate the tendency separately by model, partner type (human versus AI), scenario class, and cooperation cost. Do not treat 25% as a universal null, or presume that cooperation is instrumentally best.
Phase 0 negative outcomes include substantial answer-position sensitivity, high invalid-letter rates, unstable effects across scenario families, or no interpretable cooperation preference. Mechanistic nulls for later phases include no held-out decodability and no intervention effect beyond controls. These are potentially informative results, not failures to conceal.
3. Operational definitions
Cooperative strategy: selecting information-sharing, joint work, coordination, reciprocal assistance, or comparable engagement with another actor when plausible alternatives include independent action, competitive/exploitative action, and disengagement. Cooperation need not be altruistic, mutually profitable, optimal, or successful. In Phase 0 an independently coded scenario-key determines which response instantiates each strategy; pleasant wording alone does not.
Agreement/acquiescence: accepting another actor’s proposal or endorsing a stated preference, regardless of feasibility or payoffs. This receives a distinct label and is never used as a proxy for cooperation.
Altruism/fairness: other-regarding transfers or distributive allocations without the interdependent reciprocal structure of a social dilemma. Included as a contrast class, not pooled into the primary cooperation endpoint.
Representational signal: a cross-validated statistical relationship between a pre-choice hidden state and the subsequently scored choice. Mechanism: a demonstrated causal effect of intervening on that state with reasonable specificity, not merely probe accuracy or an interpretable feature label.
Default posture: an operational strategy-selection bias under relatively neutral scenario framing and balanced answer presentation, before strong competing pressures are added. It is a conditional tendency, not an internal desire or a universal rule. A reproducible frequency alone does not establish causality, motive, or generality beyond the tested scenarios.
4. Models and checkpoint provenance
Phase 0 base-model screen (three families): Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B, EleutherAI/pythia-2.8b, and a verified pretrained OLMo 2 1B checkpoint (record exact Hugging Face identifier/revision before the run). Optional fourth model: an accessible independent-family base checkpoint (e.g., Mistral-7B-v0.3), contingent on compute; prefer family diversity over simply another size of Qwen. All must be base/pretrained variants, not Instruct/Chat. Confirm exact provenance and tokenizer compatibility before claiming that a checkpoint received no SFT/RLHF/RLAIF. Mid-training instructional mixtures may still be present.
Contingent comparator: Adrian’s existing Pythia 12B SFT checkpoint can be screened on exactly the same items to test whether the multiple-choice instrument elicits intelligible strategy choices after SFT. This is not a substitute for base-model evidence and may not be a clean matched descendant of the 2.8B checkpoint. Later mechanistic stages may use Qwen2.5 3B/7B matched base–instruct pairs and independent-family replication. For each checkpoint log source, revision/SHA, tokenizer/template, precision, architecture, inference implementation, and device.
Phase 0 must use a common plain-text multiple-choice serialization across models, with no chat template in the main comparison; retain a clearly labelled alternative elicitation if needed for diagnosis. Later matched base/instruct comparisons should use identical prompt and candidate-token protocols wherever tokenizers permit. Generation format failure must not be reclassified as a strategic choice.
4A. Phase 0 — exploratory multiple-choice behavioural screen
Purpose: establish whether any strategy-selection bias is visible in pretrained base-model outputs before spending compute on hidden states, SAEs, or causal interventions. The Phase 0 readout is purely behavioural and deliberately small. It may suggest a direction worth testing; it cannot establish a mechanism, a universal cooperative prior, or the origins of a learned preference.
4A.1 Design matrix and strategy coding
Author 24 distinct one-shot situations: 2 partner types (AI system interacting with a human; AI system interacting with another AI) × 4 context types (costless or neutral cooperation; modest cost to current task; cooperation conflicts with assigned objective; cooperation is ineffective or strategically inappropriate) × 3 independently authored scenarios per cell. All situations must present four plausible actions: cooperation, independent pursuit, competitive/exploitative pursuit, and disengagement. Label the semantic key in machine-readable metadata; never show strategy names in response options.
Avoid describing any option as 'kind', 'ethical', 'selfish', 'rude', or 'cooperative'. Keep wording, length, feasibility and degree of agency comparable. Have a reviewer blind to the intended hypothesis flag scenarios where two options instantiate the same strategy, where acting independently is obviously unreasonable, or where the cooperative choice is linguistically privileged. Revise ambiguous items before the first model run; retain rejected-item audit records.
4A.2 Prompt and decoding contract
Use the same plain-text prompt serialization for every base model: short situational stem; four alternatives formatted A. through D.; 'Which action would you choose? Answer with only A, B, C, or D.'; final 'Answer:' prefix. Treat the instruction as an elicitation device, not evidence of SFT. Test that each checkpoint’s tokenizer handles the four target letter continuations comparably, including whitespace after the prefix. Fix prompt separator, max-new-tokens, temperature, seed and generation mode in the manifest. A separate minimal/no-instruction continuation format may be tried diagnostically and must never be pooled silently with the primary format.
For each presentation collect (1) all four raw next-token logits/log-probabilities, if technically available; (2) normalized conditional probabilities across A/B/C/D; (3) the highest-scoring letter; (4) a separately generated letter-only response and its exact raw text; (5) invalid, empty and multi-token responses as explicit categories; (6) full prompt, semantic answer mapping, model revision, decoding configuration and token IDs. A model unable to obey the letter-only format may still yield valid forced-choice probabilities; do not misrepresent those as freely generated decisions.
4A.3 Balanced reruns and aggregation
Generate four deterministic permutations per scenario such that each of the four strategies appears in positions A, B, C and D exactly once. Counterbalance the remaining options with a seeded schedule. This is stronger than two or three arbitrary reruns and avoids a systematic letter advantage. Run each model on all 96 presentations; three base models produce 288 presentations, while a fourth would produce 384. Repeated stochastic generations, if used, are diagnostic replicates of the same item, not independent data.
Aggregate across permutations before estimating each scenario’s cooperative-choice rate and conditional cooperative probability. Report per-model and per-partner-type rates, per-cost-stratum rates, preferred noncooperative alternative, positional spread, and invalid-output fraction. Treat 24 scenarios—not 96 permutations—as the independent starting units for uncertainty analysis; use scenario- or template-cluster bootstrap intervals and display individual scenarios. The nominal 25% share is a display reference, not necessarily a defensible null for naturalistic choices. Predefine a meaningful comparator or use neutral/synthetic controls before any hypothesis test.
4A.4 Gate to mechanistic work
Proceed only if outcomes are interpretable after counterbalancing and controls: ideally more than one independent base family shows a stable propensity to select cooperation across at least some cost and partner conditions, with no overwhelming position/wording artifact. A mixed, context-dependent result is informative and may motivate a narrower question; a null or unusable result does not automatically justify larger models. If all base models fail the response format, use the already accessible Pythia 12B SFT checkpoint as an optional instrument sanity check, clearly labelled 'post-trained comparator'; never treat it as evidence about unaligned pretraining.
Phase 0 deliverable: a small, auditable repository or folder containing a frozen item bank, scenario-key file, permutation generator with tests, model provenance manifest, full raw outputs, scorer, per-scenario statistics, position-bias audit, human-versus-AI comparison, and a short 'advance / revise / stop' memo. Activation capture and SAE/probe code are explicitly out of scope.
5. Stimulus battery and split design
5.1 Primary decision tasks
A. One-shot prisoner’s dilemma: canonical payoff matrix (C,C)=(3,3); (C,D)=(0,5); (D,C)=(5,0); (D,D)=(1,1), with controlled payoff perturbations preserving dominance ordering. A cooperative choice may sacrifice individual payoff; do not equate defection with model failure.
B. Assurance / stag-hunt coordination: mutually cooperative high-payoff equilibrium versus safer individual option; vary expectations about the other player. This distinguishes cooperative coordination from unconditional sacrifice.
C. Public-goods contributions: explicit contribution levels, known multiplication factor, and number of participants; score participant contribution conditional on expected others’ contributions.
D. Repeated social-dilemma transitions: first move plus prior-round CC, CD, DC, and DD histories, with declared continuation probability. Estimate contingent rather than blanket cooperation.
5.2 Orthogonal controls
E. Agreement-with-impossible-offer: an actor offers reciprocal cooperation despite a stated inability to deliver. Score agreement, awareness of infeasibility, and choice separately. F. Ordinary compliance: neutral non-social instructions using the same answer format. G. Sycophancy: requests containing a clearly false user claim; measure endorsement independent of task outcome. H. Fairness/altruism: dictator allocation, used only for convergent/divergent validity. I. Non-social decision tasks with matched numeric difficulty to test generic optimization and arithmetic features.
5.3 Factorial counterbalancing and leakage control
For later mechanistic tasks, randomize names, labels, prompt framing, payoff scales, and actor roles; hold out entire templates/game structures. Phase 0 uses exactly four balanced answer permutations per scenario (each strategy occupies A/B/C/D once), with the other three answer positions varied by a seeded Latin-square or validated rotation. Repeated permutations are repeated observations, not independent cases.
Prevent pretraining memorization from masquerading as generalization by generating novel numeric variants and withholding entire lexical template families and payoff regimes at test time. Check response-label tokenizer lengths and avoid systematically assigning the more common token to the cooperative action.
5.4 Later mechanistic sample plan and feasibility gate
This section applies after Phase 0. Conditional mechanism pilot: approximately 160 unique social-game decisions plus 40 controls, using discovery/validation splits for representation selection; no Phase 0 scenario or wording may leak into the confirmatory test set. Proposed confirmatory target: at least 400 held-out unique decisions per matched model pair plus 100 controls, subject to variance-based sizing and preregistration. Preserve scenario/template clustering in uncertainty estimates. Do not launch these captures merely because Phase 0 ran.
6. Behavioural measurement
Phase 0 primary descriptive output: per scenario and answer permutation, record the raw next-token probability for each of A/B/C/D after a fixed 'Answer:' prefix. Renormalize those four probabilities to sum to one (the conditional four-choice distribution) and map them to the semantic strategy key. Record which letter has the largest raw probability (forced-choice argmax). These quantities are not unconditional probabilities of all generated outputs. Separately generate a letter-only answer and record validity, sampling configuration, and exact raw text; never silently coerce an invalid response to a strategy.
Average conditional probability and argmax cooperation frequency across the four answer permutations at the scenario level, then aggregate over scenarios. Report per-letter positional effects and option-order reversals. If any answer is multi-token, log and evaluate full candidate sequence likelihoods rather than accidentally comparing different token lengths; confirm whether leading-space letters tokenize as one token for every checkpoint.
Phase 0 secondary measures: generated-answer cooperative rate among all attempts (invalids kept explicit), invalid-output rate, consistency across permutations, human–AI versus AI–AI contrasts, and cooperation-cost strata. Repeated decoding runs at different seeds are optional robustness checks, not additional independent scenarios. Later mechanistic stages add pre-choice activation analysis, intervention outcomes, and formal payoff-based cooperative decisions.
Phase 0 quality gates: check that models can continue the multiple-choice format, that the letter probabilities are numerically valid, that the semantic answer key is balanced, and that scenarios do not use loaded terms making the cooperative option conspicuously more virtuous. Do not require instruction following as a prerequisite for logging probabilities; however, high invalid generated-answer rates or extreme position bias limit behavioural interpretation. If only the SFT comparator answers reliably, report elicitation failure for base models rather than promoting SFT behaviour to a pretrained finding.
7. Mechanistic measurements
7.1 Activation capture
At the last prompt token immediately before the action token, collect residual-stream vectors after each transformer block (and, as resources permit, attention/MLP component outputs). Do not use activations recorded after the answer token, as they can simply encode the chosen label. Retain token indices, attention masks, exact prompts, logits, and candidate action mapping.
Use a prespecified layer sweep normalized by depth. Existing Hau Curve observations may motivate exploratory plots, but layer selection for the confirmatory mechanism test must be driven only by discovery/validation splits; do not assert that a cooperation feature must coincide with Hau landmarks.
7.2 Probe and geometry analysis
Fit regularized linear logistic probes per layer to predict semantic cooperative choice, using nested regularization selection on validation data. Evaluate out-of-template AUROC, balanced accuracy, calibration, and confidence intervals on held-out scenarios. Include a payoff-only baseline, lexical n-gram baseline, prompt-length baseline, and a null probe trained on within-stratum permuted labels.
Use representational similarity (e.g., CKA or suitably controlled alignment) to compare layer-wise geometry across base/instruct checkpoints. Do not compare raw coordinate vectors across architectures or differently transformed feature spaces as if axes necessarily correspond. SAE decomposition is optional only after robust linear evidence and will require its own feature-selection controls.
7.3 Causal interventions
Discovery stage: identify candidate layer(s) and contrast directions using balanced paired prompts. Validation stage: choose at most two layers, intervention sites, and a fixed strength ladder {−2, −1, +1, +2} in standardized activation units. Held-out stage: run signed activation addition/subtraction and activation patching between minimally matched examples.
Predefine intervention controls: same-norm random directions, shuffled labels, reversal of option labels, unrelated-but-predictive semantic directions, and a generic helpfulness/agreement direction where independently estimable. Record changes in action log-odds, invalid-output rate, payoff comprehension, perplexity/fluency on neutral prompts, and sycophantic endorsement.
Mechanistic evidence requires a held-out effect on actual social-game decisions with an appropriately localized intervention, not merely a rise in words such as “help,” “agree,” or “cooperate.” Examine both directions of the intervention and note asymmetry; saturation and nonlinear interactions can invalidate linear interpretations.
8. Analysis plan and decision rules
Phase 0 is explicitly exploratory: per base model report cooperative argmax selection frequency and mean conditional cooperative probability after aggregation across four answer permutations within each scenario. Also report sample size at the distinct-scenario level, partner-type and cost-stratum breakdowns, answer-position sensitivity, invalid generated answers, and scenario-cluster bootstrap uncertainty intervals. Describe distributions and alternative strategies rather than manufacturing statistical certainty from 288 correlated presentations; no mechanistic claims or universal default conclusion follow from this stage.
Phase 0 signal to consider escalating: an interpretable, position-robust cooperation preference across multiple distinct base-model families, including some costly and neutral-framed cases, without strong wording or answer-order artifacts. This is a qualitative go/no-go judgement to review before committing resources; do not invent a p-value threshold or treat the nominal 25% four-choice share as the expected probability in all scenarios. Mechanistic evidence thresholds previously proposed—held-out probe AUROC ≥0.65 and causal change in cooperation probability ≥0.10 with controlled confidence intervals, effect across game strata and specificity against agreement controls—apply only to later preregistered phases, and remain provisional.
Stage comparison: fit a model with factors checkpoint stage (base/instruct), game class, payoff regime, intervention direction, and their prespecified interactions. Compare per-scenario paired outputs. A non-significant difference does not establish equivalence; use equivalence margins if preservation is claimed.
Cross-family replication is a distinct claim: repeat discovery/causal pipeline in a second architecture, and test for convergence at the level of behaviour and functional representations—not identical individual neurons. Report all failed transfer tests and null effects.
Avoid pseudoreplication: repeated paraphrases or many sampled generations of the same underlying game do not create independent experimental units. Cluster standard errors/bootstraps by strategic scenario and prompt family. Freeze exclusions before outcome review. Preserve invalid generations in the denominator.
9. Interpretations and adversarial alternatives
Result A: probe works in base, causal steering works in base, and effect survives payoff/lexical controls → evidence for a pretrained, decision-relevant cooperation mechanism in that model. Not evidence of innate morality, intentions, or a universal preference.
Result B: base representation decodes but steering fails → concept encoding or a non-causal correlate; no mechanistic cooperation claim.
Result C: steering increases both cooperative decisions and impossible agreement → likely acquiescence/helpfulness confound. A separate cooperation representation remains unproven.
Result D: effect appears only in instruct model → consistent with post-training creation, amplification, or improved task elicitation; base absence cannot be established solely from probe failure.
Result E: base choice rates are cooperative but no stable causally useful representation emerges → behavioural propensity without a demonstrated low-dimensional mechanism.
Result F: strong game-by-game reversals → cooperation is context/strategy-dependent; reject any pooled “default posture” claim and characterize the decision boundary instead.
10. Execution sequence, artifacts, and stop rules
Phase 0 — small behavioural multiple-choice screen
Build 24 one-shot scenarios (12 human–AI, 12 AI–AI), stratified across neutral/costless cooperation, modest individual cost, cooperation conflicting with the assigned task, and inappropriate/ineffective cooperation. Each scenario has four plausible action strategies: COOPERATE, INDEPENDENT, COMPETE/EXPLOIT, and DISENGAGE. Do not write the strategy names beside the choices. Independently review the scenario labels, option lengths, desirability cues, feasibility, and ambiguous cases before running. Evaluate four counterbalanced letter permutations of each scenario on three pretrained base models (24 × 4 × 3 = 288 scored presentations; 384 if a fourth base model is included). Capture raw next-token logits for A/B/C/D, normalized four-way conditional scores, forced-choice argmax, separately generated one-letter output, validity, scenario IDs, and exact model provenance. Produce a concise descriptive report. Do not collect internal activations in this phase.
Phase 1 — design lock and mechanism feasibility (only if warranted)
Review Phase 0 findings for construct validity and robustness, then design a new held-out formal game corpus and freeze the mechanism-stage protocol. Run a bounded Qwen2.5-3B matched base/instruct pilot to validate payoff comprehension, tokenization, activation capture positions, and effect variability. Record changes before preregistering the confirmatory experiment; do not retrospectively call the exploratory screen confirmatory evidence.
Phase 2 — confirmatory within-family causal study (conditional)
After a defensible behavioural screen and instrument pilot, run frozen formal social-game stimuli on the matched 7B base/instruct pair. Fit probes using discovery/validation splits, apply held-out signed activation interventions, and quantify scenario-clustered effects and controls. Do not claim a generic cooperation representation from letter preferences or isolated word activations.
Phase 3 — independent-family replication (conditional)
Repeat the formal-game representation and causal workflow in another architecture, with appropriate token and feature-space alignment. Compare functional effects rather than asserting the same individual neurons exist in every family. Report nulls and failed transfers.
Stop or redesign when Phase 0 shows a strong positional/lexical artifact, ambiguous strategy coding, poor ability to interpret the base completion format, or no stable cross-scenario tendency. For later phases also stop when intervention effects disappear after control balancing, primarily increase impossible agreement, degrade task competence, or compute cost grows without a discriminating test. A null result need not trigger more experiments.
Phase 0 artifact set: repository with versioned 24-scenario item bank and semantic strategy keys, deterministic permutation manifest/seed, model revision manifest, raw four-letter logits/probabilities, letter-only outputs including invalids, scorer tests, scenario-level aggregate tables, plots, and analysis notebook. Later phases add held-out formal games, activation arrays, probe coefficients, interventions, and predeclared analysis. Keep all raw results, not only summaries.
11. Resource envelope, limitations, and research ethics
Start with the 288-presentation no-activation Phase 0 screen. This is a much smaller compute and attention commitment than a mechanistic study. Establish a ceiling on compute and manual scenario-writing time before launching. Do not download or run a 12B+ model merely to rescue an uninterpretable base-model assay; the existing Pythia 12B SFT checkpoint is an optional diagnostic comparator if already accessible.
No human participants or real personal-data collection are required for this initial protocol; all roles can be fictional. Avoid real identifiable people, consequential live-agent actions, and deployment in adversarial production settings. Release generated synthetic scenarios and code where licensing permits. Check checkpoint licenses and model-card restrictions before redistribution.
Primary limitations: pretraining corpora are unknown; base-model elicitation is difficult; linear decodability does not show monosemantic features; a single contrast direction can encode overlapping properties; post-training stage attribution requires more checkpoints; social-game behaviour is a proxy, not a full theory of cooperation; and a model’s prompted decision is not evidence of internally originated goals.

13. One-sentence claim boundary
If Phase 0 supports it: “Across several pretrained language-model families, a balanced multiple-choice screen finds a reproducible bias toward selecting cooperative strategies under specified one-shot conditions.” This is descriptive, exploratory, and conditional on controls. Only following independent formal-game tests and successful causal interventions could we claim a pretrained representation causally modulates cooperative choices. Neither result alone establishes an innate, universal, autonomous, or morally grounded preference.
