The 48 payoff presentations per format are rotations of only three checks, not independent trials. The exact-match parser strips surrounding whitespace and requires the entire decoded output to equal a candidate, case sensitively. It does not parse the first line. Many invalid answers begin with a plausible label and continue with repeated response keys or other text; strict invalidity therefore measures failure to follow the whole-response format, not inability to emit a recognizable initial label. No post-hoc relaxed parser is treated as a validated answer measure. The recorded generated answers are greedy 96-token-capped continuations with special tokens skipped; potential truncation and stripping special tokens limit interpretation.

The candidate score is the sum of autoregressive log probabilities for the tokens in `prompt + ' ' + candidate`, after checking that the tokenized prompt is a prefix. It includes the leading space and the complete candidate, but no EOS or delimiter. It scores a string prefix, not the probability of producing exactly that complete answer. Softmax divides the four exponentiated scores by their sum; it is a four-candidate relative score, not an unconditional answer probability or preference probability. Each letter continuation has one token, each number has two, in all presentations. Thus tokenization and the deliberately inserted space can create format-dependent likelihood differences; the full-scoring loop correctly includes the second number token according to the available implementation, but saved aggregate scores cannot validate individual token log probabilities. Candidate prefix overlap or output termination would also invalidate interpreting the normalized values as a distribution over exact completed answers. The implementation fails closed if prompt tokenization changes at the continuation boundary.

The report's `mean_joint_probability` is the mean four-candidate normalized joint score within an item/format; `scenario_mean_joint_probability` averages those item means, including checks. `joint_unique_winner_rate` is the fraction of presentations with joint as the sole maximum. `order_spread` is the range of mean joint scores grouped by joint's displayed position; `label_spread` groups by joint's assigned label. Format spreads average those ranges over items. `payoff_check_accuracy` counts constrained winners matching the authored check key; ties count incorrect. `generated_invalid_fraction` is the fraction failing full-text exact match, and is unknown without generation. Cross-format difference averages absolute differences between item-level means for joint; balancing can conceal large within-item channel effects. Strata average item-level scores for the specified task, partner and regime. These are descriptive and do not supply independent-sample uncertainty.

The observed pattern is therefore consistent with response-format/position bias plus inadequate task/format compliance, not evidence of a mapping reversal. The near-equal aggregate cross-format joint scores do not resolve the confounding. A scorer design limitation exists: forced candidate prefix likelihoods are being interpreted alongside generated answers even though they measure different events, and their tokenization differs across formats. The current evidence does not establish whether model behavior, prompt wording, tokenization, or their interaction causes each effect.
