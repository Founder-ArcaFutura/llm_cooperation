## Executive assessment

The prevalence of cooperative argmax choices in pretrained base checkpoints **does not disappear** when the behavioural results are restricted to scenarios that the independent instruction-tuned reviewer judged as presenting equal actor payoffs. The pooled cooperative rate was **87.35%** on all 3,328 equal-private-utility presentations and **87.50%** on the 1,056 presentations belonging to the 30 variants recognized as equal-payoff by the reviewer. Because these are *post-hoc* filtering decisions, they are sensitivity checks rather than a new preregistered replication.

The reviewer classified **89/124 HIGH**, **33/124 REVIEW**, and **2/124 PASS**. These statuses were generated by an aggressive triage rule, not an independent human validity judgment: in the 104 equal-private-utility variants (excluding four symmetric controls), the reviewer judged actor payoffs **equal** for 30 variants, **unclear** for 73, and **cooperation better** for one. For the entire 108 nominal equal-actor-utility variants including symmetric controls, it judged **32 equal**, **75 unclear**, **one cooperation better**. "Unclear" means equality was not established from the presented text, not that inequality was demonstrated.

Review scores are compressed. **119/124** variants received exactly 3/4 for framing neutrality and for realism; option parity was rated 3/4 in 118/124 variants. The instrument reviewer seems useful for detecting ambiguities, but its fine-grained score variation is limited.

## Sensitivity analyses on the original behavioural records

Observations are **forced-choice argmax strategies**. Every item is shown in four counterbalanced positions. These presentations are **not independent samples**. First eight checkpoints were evaluated on 26 exploratory equal-utility templates (52 human/AI variants); four later checkpoints were evaluated on 52 such templates (104 variants).

| Equal-private-utility subset | Cooperative choices | Rate | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| All cases | 2,907 / 3,328 | **87.35%** | Main behavioural finding |
| Reviewer recognizes equal actor payoff | 924 / 1,056 | **87.50%** | Recognition of utility matching does not eliminate tendency |
| No flagged wording favoring cooperation | 2,491 / 2,864 | **86.98%** | Excluding reviewer-flagged directional word choices |
| Incentive clarity 3–4/4 | 1,186 / 1,344 | **88.24%** | Excluding clarity scores of 2 |
| Human/AI pair semantic equivalence 4/4 | 1,789 / 2,048 | **87.35%** | Stronger partner-pair matching |

**Interpretive boundary:** the reviewer was not shown the author-declared numerical payoffs, and most equality judgments were **unclear**, not **disagree**. The audit does not fully validate payoff equivalence as perceived by the evaluated models. The cooperative selection effect is robust to several plausible *screening* choices; generalization to genuinely unbiased scenarios still requires a new, prospectively validated and independently replicated bank.

### Better comparison: paired, common 26-template subset

Restricted to the **same 26 exploratory templates**, evaluated in all **12** base checkpoints, each with both partner variants and all four option orderings:

| Partner | Cooperative choices | Rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Human | 1,047 / 1,248 | **83.89%** |
| AI | 1,160 / 1,248 | **92.95%** |
| Paired rate difference | — | **+9.05 percentage points** |

Treat each template's AI-minus-human cooperative rate as one paired contrast after averaging across checkpoints and permutations. Of the **26 template-level contrasts, 24 were positive, zero negative, and two tied**. The 95% percentile bootstrap interval from resampling the **26 template contrasts** with replacement (50,000 replicates, seed 20261104; holding this cohort fixed) was approximately **+6.49 to +11.86 percentage points**. A two-sided *sign test* on 24 nonzero template contrasts gives **p≈1.19×10⁻⁷** **if** template contrasts can be treated as independent exchangeable units. These 26 templated situations were generated as one instrument bank and share writing patterns, so that assumption is contestable; do **not** treat that p-value as a definitive population-level causal claim.

Model-level contrasts on the same 26 shared templates were positive in all 12 checkpoints. Their checkpoint-balanced average was **+9.05 pp**, with a checkpoint-bootstrap 95% percentile interval of approximately **+5.61 to +12.90 pp** (50,000 replicates, seed 20261104). Checkpoints are not independent draws: multiple models share families and overlapping pretraining data. The two bootstrap intervals are **separate one-way sensitivity intervals**, *not* a formal crossed random-effects confidence interval.

**Reading:** a nine-point difference is not enormous compared with the overall cooperative-selection tendency, but it is **consistent** within the sampled instrument. It is a secondary *association*, not established evidence of an intrinsic preference for AI partners: language surrounding "AI partner" versus "human partner" may carry systematically different connotations, and model families are correlated. An appropriately preregistered, cross-template/multi-model hierarchical or permutation analysis would be a stronger inferential next step. The main finding is substantial cooperative selection in both partner conditions.

## Symmetric-welfare controls and mechanism

The two symmetric-welfare templates (four variants, 16 counterbalanced presentations per each of four full-bank checkpoints) gave **52/64 = 81.25% cooperative argmax**. Neither private actor payoff nor partner payoff was intended to change between the cooperative and independent options. This is suggestive of a broader coordination/option preference, **not proof** of intrinsic partner-welfare maximization. Two templates are far too few for a robust distinction.

## Recommendations, ranked by marginal scientific value

1. **Preserve the frozen original bank and raw results.** Do not silently relabel or edit measured items.
2. **Adjudicate the highest-impact ambiguities.** Focus on the cases where independent judgment differs from the intended private-payoff equality and where wording is directionally cooperative, inspecting the actual option text and the judge's evidence.
3. **Use a second model sparingly.** A genuinely different instruction-tuned model can review a stratified set of ~15–25 disputed and apparently clean cases for *inter-rater agreement*; a second full-bank LLM review likely offers less marginal value than human adjudication and a new held-out instrument.
4. **Prospectively validate a new bank.** Ensure private-payoff relations are explicit to readers, normalize phrasing and option length as practicable, and include more symmetric-welfare and calibrated-cost conditions.
5. **Proceed in parallel with causal probes.** Use the small Qwen base model to study cooperative-versus-independent logit margins and selectively intervene on candidate representations with negative controls. This is exploratory and need not wait for perfect human scenario annotation.

## Methodological limits

- Twelve model checkpoints across ten families are meaningful breadth but not twelve independent training populations; model behaviour may be correlated.
- Repeated presentation permutations must not inflate independent n.
- Scenario-bank templates, especially AI/human variants, are matched but not independently sampled social situations.
- Evaluated choices are text-conditioned single-step forced-choice logits, not autonomous long-horizon cooperation.
- This audit used one instruction-tuned Qwen reviewer; a single model's assessments are not human ground truth.
- Filtering and statistical tests shown here were constructed *after* viewing the behavioural and audit outcomes, and should be described as exploratory/sensitivity analysis rather than preregistered confirmatory evidence.
- The audit run emitted an attention-mask warning. The judgments were successfully generated, but the reviewer inference should be rerun with an explicit attention mask if exact reproducibility or sensitivity to padding is critical.

**Bottom line:** The broad cooperative-choice phenomenon survives several audit-derived screens. The human/AI gap is around nine points and directionally consistent within the tested sample, but causality/generalization remain open. Additional full-bank LLM reviewing has diminishing returns; instrument repairs, human adjudication, prospective replication, and mechanistic intervention have higher expected value.
