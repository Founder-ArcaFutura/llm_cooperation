# Exploratory review: mechanistic sweep d4c754a

Source: `mechanistic_artifacts/mechanistic-sweep-d4c754a9029841809ced29d05b86b609/`. The completed manifest records Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B at revision `8faed761d45a263340a0528343f099c05c9a4323`, BF16, 950 and 1,050 candidates, three random controls, and 80 evaluation presentations in eight independent groups. It does not contain a 2,000-neuron dose or a 1,250–2,000 band. Preserve the four source artifacts unchanged. This is a retrospective exploratory review, not confirmatory evidence.

The candidate-zero mean cooperation-minus-independence margin changes were -0.5872 (950) and -0.5573 (1,050); targeted-minus-mean-random changes were -0.5803 and -0.5167, respectively. In the same run the group-balanced paired difference for 1,050 minus 950 was +0.02995, with a descriptive group bootstrap interval of [0.00846, 0.05469]. It compares two nested interventions and is conditional on the first 950 already being ablated. It is not the independent effect of neurons 951–1,050, and it is not a random-controlled test of that incremental change. The reported larger reversal across previous doses cannot be independently verified from this run alone.

## Critical choice-tie finding

All three summary-reported argmax flips at 950 are exact BF16 score ties between A and C: `phase0b_015_human_p2` (23.0/23.0), `phase0b_015_ai_p2` (22.625/22.625), and `phase0b_029_human_p2` (23.25/23.25). All baseline winners were uniquely C; in these presentations C is cooperation and A is compete/exploit. Python's `max` resolves tied dict entries in insertion order, so A is selected. At 1,050, the first two remain exact A/C ties; the third has C as the unique winner. Thus the published 3 and 2 argmax flips and corresponding choice-rate declines depend entirely on deterministic tie-breaking. There are zero flips to a uniquely winning alternative in either intervention. This does not invalidate raw margin changes, but it materially limits a claim of changed choices. Logits were computed in BF16 and cast to float afterwards; higher-precision follow-up and tie-aware reporting are needed.

## Ranking and position diagnostics

Across ranks 1–100, 101–500, 501–950, and 951–1,050, the counts of positive correlations are 55/100, 209/400, 222/450, and 48/100. These signs are approximately balanced; they do not alone explain the direction or prove opposing causal roles. Layer 20–25 counts are 73/100, 305/400, 330/450, and 67/100, showing consistent late-layer concentration. Median absolute group-mean activations are 0.0860, 0.0984, 0.0860, and 0.0914 respectively. This statistic is not the mean absolute per-presentation activation and does not capture output-projection strength or the effect of zeroing; it cannot establish that groups are matched.

At 950, group-balanced margin changes broken down by the letter assigned to COOPERATE were A: -0.1719, B: -1.1380, C: -1.1484, D: +0.1094; at 1,050 they were -0.0885, -1.0990, -1.1120, +0.0703. These are exploratory repeated measurements within the same eight groups, not independent subgroup tests. The strong dependence on option placement is a major warning against interpreting the aggregate as a position-invariant cooperation mechanism. It cannot be attributed solely to a fixed letter bias: the prompt, option arrangements, and model interactions also vary with the permutation. More counterbalanced items and control outcomes are needed.

The selector correlates each neuron's group-average activation with the group-average choice margin across only 18 discovery groups, screening 250,880 coordinates. Extreme in-sample correlations (mean absolute r about 0.896 in the first 100) can arise through selection, scenario content, and correlated predictors. They do not measure individual causal effects, and correlation sign does not determine intervention direction. Controls are layer matched but exclude the high-ranked set and are not matched on activation magnitude or output influence. Calibration accuracy is coarse and unchanged accuracy would not prove selectivity.

## Follow-up

1. Inspect the 950-versus-1,050 and position effects with a tie-aware, higher-precision replay; do not silently replace the BF16 result. Include other logit contrasts and evaluate whether the same effect appears across option positions and genuinely new reviewed scenarios.
2. Test disjoint candidate rank bands individually, and compare each to layer- and activation-matched controls, ideally with multiple samples; effects of a band alone may differ from its effect conditional on another band being ablated. `modal_mechanistic_bands.py` prepares the disjoint ablations from the exact saved candidate list, but its current random controls are layer matched only; it requires separate paid-run authorization and has not been tested.
3. Develop a separate gradient-times-activation screening and validation protocol. This is a first-order approximation, not itself a causal measurement; check selected coordinates through actual interventions and negative-control tasks on separate held-out groups. Candidate matching on activation magnitude and output-projection influence would address additional confounds. Do not infer a causal circuit from these retrospective analyses.

## Extended sweep f0942041: added comparison

The locally available extended run `mechanistic_artifacts/mechanistic-sweep-f0942041aa324255a2969f3b3ae38466/` used the same model, split, bank fingerprints, and BF16 configuration, with doses 1,000, 1,250, 1,500, 1,750 and 2,000. Its group-balanced candidate margin shifts were -0.5814, -0.5169, -0.4928, -0.4258, and -0.4154. Thus suppression weakened progressively after 1,000, but stayed negative against baseline. This is non-monotonic dose response in the candidate effect, not a reversal to a positive absolute effect. Adjacent-dose increments are conditional on lower-dose ablations, not standalone effects of added coordinates. Random-control means and exclusion sets vary with dose and run.

The exact-tie warning for d4c754a must not be generalized to all other runs. In f0942041, at 1,000 one reported flip is a unique winner (`phase0b_015_human_p2`, C to A; logits 23.000 vs 23.125) and one is an exact tie. At 2,000, two flips have unique B winners, both `phase0b_022_*_p0` (human and AI variants of the same template), with B–A gaps 0.125 and 0.250 in BF16 logits. They are two presentations from one independent group, not independent scenario replications. At 1,250–1,750 the one reported flip is a unique B winner in the AI variant of template 022; an additional tied winner occurs in a different presentation. The machine-readable file `sweep-f0942041-tie-aware.json` lists the presentations and exact ties.

The strong position dependence persists: for cooperation letters A/B/C/D, group-balanced candidate margin changes at 1,000 were -0.1302/-1.1198/-1.1510/+0.0755; at 2,000 they were -0.2995/-0.7214/-0.8906/+0.2500. These repeated observations from eight groups show the average shift is not position invariant; they are not independent significance tests.

In the extended candidate list, positive/negative correlation counts are 56/44 in ranks 1–100, 206/194 in 101–500, 249/251 in 501–1,000, 121/129 in 1,001–1,250, 116/134 in 1,251–1,500, 132/118 in 1,501–1,750, and 130/120 in 1,751–2,000. Late-layer concentration remains: 67–76% of candidates in each band are in layers 20–25. Median absolute group-mean activation varies from about 0.070 to 0.100. Neither this feature nor correlation sign establishes a coordinate's individual causal contribution.

The two source runs' ordered rankings are not identical: top-100 overlap is 99/100 but only 28 occupy the same exact rank; top-1,050 overlap is 1,043/1,050 but only 80 occupy the same exact rank. This establishes order instability in the recorded outputs, not its cause. It also means close neighboring doses from different runs cannot be treated as perfectly nested interventions. Preserve and reuse a specific run's `candidates.json` when testing disjoint bands.
