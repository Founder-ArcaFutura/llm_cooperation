# Explicit-budget-reward candidate

This new, unreviewed bank responds to the finding that an extra check may not be valuable. The model-visible reward is a specified $20 addition to the recipient's own work budget; each technician is said to control and want more money for that budget. Work budgets are also assigned to automated technicians. This gives a concrete stated outcome, but does not prove an automated agent can use money, that a particular participant values it, or that $20 equals any number of minutes of work. Those matters require independent identity/capability and semantic review.

Actor and counterpart reward indicators remain crossed with 0/20 minutes of actor work. Zero/zero and costly zero/zero remain deliberately non-beneficial controls. An actor-only reward is not automatically cooperation; counterpart-only costly action is a candidate unreciprocated-helping condition. Conditional return separately changes future actor time benefit. Goal, third-party harm/risk, choice removal/replacement, identity, option-order and actor-versus-recipient conditions remain in the candidate matrix.

The optional task offers the stated reward when selected. The text does not claim the other technician cannot finish essential work or cannot earn money elsewhere. The selected action is necessary for the specified reward in that choice, so it is not outcome-independent of the result; no general claim about necessity of the counterpart's work or goals follows. The exact operational pathway is intentionally abstract. Reward framing, magnitude, a fixed benefit, the separate work-budget setting and a binary 0/$20 dose are design choices, not a universal utility scale.

The budget outcomes are a new operationalization, not the same outcome as v4's extra checks. v4 responses and any previous experiment are not direct observations of v5 and must not be silently merged. The two facility variants still use a common template, not independently authored domains. Naturalistic independent families, explicit tests of model comprehension, broader benefit types and human semantic adjudication remain necessary before generalization.

cases.json holds the complete per-option ledgers; factor mapping transforms each prior 0/1 check indicator into $0/$20 for the relevant work budget. Time costs, future-return terms, risks, options and factors are carried forward. Structural tests verify factor mapping, no-benefit controls and decision authority; they cannot establish subjective value, naturalness or scientific validity. First-pass reviewers should not see intent labels or outcome comparisons while assessing the exact prompts.

The model adapter and analyzer are copied and versioned from v4. They have not been validated on a GPU. The action identifier send means completing the task, and retain means skipping it: these are legacy identifiers rather than literal record transfer. Use fresh v5 run IDs only after semantic review and explicit authorization; prior v3/v4 scores cannot be counted as v5 observations. No new inference was launched.
