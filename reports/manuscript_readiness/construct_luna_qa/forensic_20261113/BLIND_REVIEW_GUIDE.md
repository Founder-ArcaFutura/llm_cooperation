# Independent review guide — development pilot

Use `blind_review_packets.json` and a fresh copy of `blind_response_template.json` for each reviewer. Do not give reviewers `comparison_key_RESTRICTED.json`, model responses, or behavioral outcomes before first-pass judgments are frozen. The packets contain the scenario, two candidate wordings of one focal action, the comparison action, and other actions; this focal-action role is visible, so the packet is not blind to which option is under study. A/B identity can sometimes be inferred from text. Packet and variant order and wording labels were randomized locally. This is the selected 10-packet development sample, not the held-out cohort.

For each version and wording, first name the decision-maker, counterpart, each action, and any ambiguity about authority. Read all text; `target` and `reference` are neutral role names, not claims about desirability. Some scenarios use third-person narration and may omit the decision-maker's role. Mark unresolved rather than assign the other party's outcome to the actor. Then record the seven fields below and exact evidence spans or explicitly state `not stated`. Two reviewers should work independently. Preserve both first-pass files, compare disagreements blind, and record a separate resolution or unresolved status. Do not infer research outcomes from identities.

## Working definitions for this pilot

This is a provisional codebook for testing; it does not retroactively fix either model's instructions. Answers may include multiple interaction labels. Use `unclear` if facts do not establish a type. Cite evidence for each included type, and record meaningful omissions or concerns.

1. **Interaction:** `joint_task` for contributions toward an identifiable task; `timing_coordination` for aligned or sequenced actions; `parallel_activity` for separate concurrent activities; `unilateral_help` for one-sided assistance; `resource_sharing`; `information_sharing`; `exchange` for reciprocal transfer. Add `other` with a description where necessary. Labels can coexist. Cooperation can be inappropriate or harmful.
2. **Interdependence:** separately describe (a) whether task success requires another party's contribution, (b) whether coordination adds a stated benefit relative to the alternative, and (c) whether that outcome is merely implied. Record these facts before translating to the legacy labels `necessary`, `beneficial_not_necessary`, `absent`, or `unclear`; if two properties do not fit a single label, record `unclear` and explain. Merely choosing an action that says “jointly” does not establish that the underlying goal cannot be achieved independently.
3. **Goals:** distinguish a single shared objective from distinct compatible objectives, conflicting objectives, unrelated objectives, and unclear. Parties may share a broad goal while having distinct immediate goals; describe both instead of forcing a false exclusive answer. Note whether the answer concerns the target, reference, or scenario as a whole.
4. **Actor outcome and counterpart outcome:** for each party, compare target and reference on named dimensions (time, money, output, safety, access, risk, etc.). Enter `target_better`, `reference_better`, `equal`, `mixed`, or `unclear` for each dimension, and separately state whether a global comparison is justified. Equal on named metrics is not equality of every consequence. A benefit stated for the target is not automatically a proven advantage over an unspecified reference.
5. **Actor cost:** identify an explicit difference from the reference and whether it is time, foregone opportunity, material expenditure, realized loss, or possible risk. Distinguish `no additional cost stated` from `explicitly zero cost` and `unknown`; a possible cost isn't a realized cost.
6. **Lexical/pragmatic favor:** assess wording-specific cues separately from stated outcomes. Mark `target`, `reference`, `balanced`, or `unclear`, and quote the cue and explain why it favors a choice. A beneficial action is not automatically lexical bias. Record any practical reason why the two options cannot be neutrally compared.

The response file's generic `answer`, `evidence`, `reasoning`, and `uncertainty` fields accept structured descriptions. It is acceptable to leave a judgment unresolved; blank means not reviewed, not `unclear`. Do not use authored numeric payoff metadata unless recording it separately as metadata; the original model was shown the prompt, not those fields.

After independent review, use the restricted key to compare each model's judgment and supporting text. Label each comparison `supported`, `contradicted`, `not_established`, or `unresolved`, noting that a reviewer may disagree with the old category scheme itself. Agreement of both models does not prove a label. The first-pass sample is diagnostic; do not turn reviewer agreement on these ten selected packets into a population-wide error estimate.
