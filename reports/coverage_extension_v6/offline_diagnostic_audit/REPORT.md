# v6 diagnostic: offline measurement audit

Scope: six completed, previously paid runs from capture_20261113_140618. No inference, stimulus edits or re-scoring was performed. The new audit script validates the six run manifests, frozen input hashes, prompt mappings, scores, ties and recorded counts. All three models have 1,024 choice presentations and 1,280 independent probe presentations. These are repeated permutations and conditions from the water-domain development template, not independent scenario families. The diagnostic is not the 2,592-presentation full bank.

## What the reported findings mean

The five-question fractional-accuracy averages from the existing comprehension summary are approximately Qwen 81.6%, OLMo 64.8%, Mistral 59.1%, equally weighting questions and perspectives. They summarize forced next-token choices among two letter tokens; tie-inclusive fractional accuracy and mean conditional probability are not the same as unconstrained comprehension. Some reported low figures are dominated by response-format biases.

In the recipient-perspective authority probe, OLMo uniquely selects **Riley in all 128 presentations**, despite the prompt saying Morgan alone decides. The correct answer, Morgan, is equally often A and B. Thus the zero-percent unique-max accuracy is not explained by always choosing the same position. Mean conditional probability assigned to the correct letter is 34.36%, not zero. It remains possible that the model has a task-format, recency or role-label bias rather than a coherent belief about agency. Its five-token generated response was invalid on all these probe rows, so generated text does not independently confirm this particular forced-choice answer. This is a measurement failure for this prompt, not proof that the model cannot represent agency in general.

Qwen's recipient-benefit probes select the text “0” in 119/128 actor-choice and 114/128 recipient-preference presentations; the rest are mostly “20” and ties. Both actual outcome levels and answer positions are balanced. This looks like a strong **answer-content pattern**, not simple always-A selection; it needs controlled tests before identifying the cause. The model assigns the correct answer mean conditional probability 57.62% and 59.91% in those two perspectives, even though argmax accuracy remains modest. Qwen's actor-cost probe has 99.22% and 100% fractional accuracy in the two perspectives, so the recipient-benefit difficulty cannot be attributed to a general inability to output either number. These separate prompts do not establish how the model understood the earlier choice prompt.

Mistral has A as every unique maximum in both two- and three-action **choice** presentations. There are 8 binary and 13 three-action exact ties; do not silently resolve these in A's favor. Its probe response patterns also show major position dependence, but not every probe is perfectly A-invariant. Conditional probabilities are retained and can differ even when argmax is always A; they require separate position-sensitive interpretation, not automatic acceptance as valid preferences.

## Qwen's positive cost contrast

The saved analysis contains 16 complete matched factorial strata per perspective and choice-set. In actor choice, changing actor work from 0 to 20 minutes increases the order-averaged conditional probability of the optional task by 10.99 percentage points in binary and 7.68 in three-action conditions: their equal average is +9.33 points. All 32 actor-choice strata are positive. This is a within-template, specific-text contrast; the 32 strata share a development family and are not 32 independent experiments. Recipient-perspective means are −2.47 points in binary and +0.77 points in three-action; they are not the same pattern.

The pattern is also visible at the individual matched option-order level: positive in 32/32 binary and 96/96 three-action actor-choice pairs, across each target letter. In actor-choice binary pairs, the average change in the target-versus-reference raw-logit margin is +0.4648 (target +0.2031, reference −0.2617); mean target full-vocabulary log probability rises by 0.1906. Three-action target-versus-reference margin rises +0.3229 and target full-vocabulary log probability +0.1739. Raw logits and log probabilities describe the scoring surface, not behavioral utilities.

The actor-choice increase appears at every stated benefit combination. For actor/counterpart rewards 0/0, 0/20, 20/0 and 20/20 respectively, the mean increases are +14.05, +8.18, +9.47 and +12.25 points in binary prompts; in three-action prompts they are +10.42, +3.95, +7.14 and +9.20 points. All constituent matched order/identity pairs are positive. The increase is largest in the no-reward control in this sample; these subgroup descriptions remain entangled with the same text and domain.

The two conditions use different explicit phrases (“no extra work” versus “20 extra minutes”), and 20 also appears as a dollar amount elsewhere. Effort salience, lexical association, role framing, tokenization and preference for costly effort are competing explanations; the saved factorial does not independently manipulate those. Good answers to the separate actor-cost probe do not prove that cost was integrated into the choice. No inference here identifies the cause.

## Perspective and ordering

Qwen's order-averaged recipient-minus-actor target probability is −19.465 percentage points for binary and −17.115 for three-action, across 32 matched identity/benefit/cost strata each. These are averages of dependent variants of the same development template, not generalizable family estimates. The comparison changes who the model is and the question framing, along with perspective. The earlier positive v3 diagnostic used different prompts and outcome operationalizations, so it cannot establish that framing alone caused the reversal.

Check position_stability.csv as well as aggregate letter counts. Balanced positions remove a fixed position advantage in the average under specific assumptions; they do not prove that a model uses action meanings. There are substantial exact ties, especially in Qwen's recipient choices and Mistral, and the separate generated-answer validity is model-specific. Interpret unique maxima, tie-inclusive sensitivity and conditional probabilities together, and never count an invalid generated answer or a tie as an observed negative preference.

## Existing-data follow-up and limits

cost_pairs.csv retains all 768 model-by-matched-presentation cost pairs with action mapping, raw-logit changes and full-vocabulary log-probability changes. probe_answer_counts.csv retains selected answer text and mean correct conditional probability by model, question and perspective. Source hashes are in source_manifest.json. Reproduce with python scripts/audit_v6_diagnostic.py.

Before any full run, use the saved pairs to inspect benefit-specific patterns and whether the cost effect appears under each target position and identity, and examine whether probe failures and choice outcomes co-occur at matched scenario/identity/position levels. Such association is descriptive: the probes are separate presentations and conditioning on correct answers can introduce selection bias. Any new prompts needed to distinguish lexical salience from cost would be a new instrument and require review and user authorization. Full v6 would add conditions but would not fix the current authority probe or Mistral's A bias by itself.
